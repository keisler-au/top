# Answer

I’ve treated each row as one respondent, numbered **R1–R11** in the order supplied, and analysed their answers across all three questions.

**Topics** describe what people discuss. **Themes** interpret the broader patterns connecting those topics. The themes below are provisional interpretations of these 11 responses.

## 2. Themes

### Theme 1: The mountain’s value depends on whether local people can access it and benefit from it

Several responses judge the project through its relationship with existing residents. Public access and free facilities are desirable; affordable housing and a non-profit approach strengthen its appeal. Gentrification and tourist-oriented development threaten that appeal.

**Evidence:** “stays publically accessible” (R1); “free, public sports facilities” (R3); “push housing prices up for current residents” (R7); “catering more to tourists and visitors” (R9).

**Interpretation:** Local benefit is a condition of support, rather than simply another requested feature. Public access, affordability and protection against displacement are distinct concerns within this theme.

### Theme 2: People imagine a place for everyday neighbourhood life

The requested interiors accommodate exercise, socialising, learning, growing food, cultural activities and quiet time. Taken together, they suggest a place people could use repeatedly for different purposes.

**Evidence:** Flexible community spaces (R5), a neighbourhood café (R7), quiet reading corners (R8), after-school programmes (R9) and local performances (R10).

**Interpretation:** The collective vision is multifunctional and rooted in daily life. This does **not** establish that every respondent wants every function; it reveals the breadth of needs the project could serve.

### Theme 3: Green identity is attractive when it connects to practical community use

Nature appears both as a distinctive identity for Charlois and as something residents could experience, cultivate and learn from.

**Evidence:** A “unique, green landmark” (R2), a “green meeting place for all ages” (R5), food growing and sustainability learning (R6), and environmental workshops for schools (R11).

**Interpretation:** The project’s environmental appeal combines symbolic value—local visibility and pride—with practical value through recreation, education and food growing.

### Theme 4: An ambitious vision raises questions about lasting viability

Some respondents focus on whether the project can be completed, maintained and accommodated within the surrounding neighbourhood.

**Evidence:** Technical feasibility and long-term maintenance costs (R8), traffic and parking (R10), and funding shortages leading to significant reductions (R11).

**Interpretation:** These concerns suggest that enthusiasm needs to be supported by a credible delivery and operating plan. They do not, by themselves, indicate opposition.

### Theme 5: Participation requires different levels and forms of commitment

Respondents offer substantially different kinds of involvement: receiving updates, contributing ideas, providing expertise, doing practical work, promoting the project and delivering future activities.

**Evidence:** Newsletter updates (R1, R4), town halls and focus groups (R3, R11), technical advice (R5), practical volunteering (R6), and running youth or sports activities (R10).

**Interpretation:** A single participation route would miss this variety. Some people want information, others want influence, and others want an active role in making or operating the space. These are stated preferences, not confirmed commitments.
